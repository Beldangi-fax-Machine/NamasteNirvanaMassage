Our Story

From Beldangi to Burlington

Nikesh Pokhrel grew up in the Beldangi refugee camp in eastern Nepal. As a young man, he watched his parents' bodies absorb years of hard labor and unrelenting stress — shoulders that never dropped, backs that never fully rested, tension that became part of who they were.

That experience set him on a path: to understand how the body holds stress, and to learn the therapeutic practices that release it. He studied how cortisol and chronic tension reshape muscle over time, and how skilled bodywork can interrupt that cycle — lowering stress hormones, restoring circulation, and returning the body to rest.

Along the way he learned something deeper: the mind and the muscles are not separate. Grief, worry, and old stress are held physically — which is why a massage can sometimes bring someone to tears. At Namaste Nirvana, that release is not an accident. It's the work.

Now, Nikesh is bringing that work home to Longwood Drive in South Burlington, Vermont — a calm, welcoming studio where every body is met with respect, skill, and time to truly let go.

Our Approach

Bodywork that treats stress at its source

Every session is built around three principles Nikesh has carried from his earliest training to today.

Mind–muscle connection

Tension is a conversation between your nervous system and your body. We work with both — slowing the breath, calming the mind, and letting the muscles follow.

Emotional release

Stress and grief live in tissue as much as in thought. When held emotion surfaces during a session — even as tears — it is met with quiet, unhurried care.

Stress & cortisol relief

Chronic stress keeps the body braced. Consistent, skilled massage helps downshift the stress response so rest, sleep, and recovery can return.
